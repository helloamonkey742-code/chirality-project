DISCLOSURE.  The paper has already been read by the project: blind_units.py's docstring (RESULTS) reports Fig. 2, Fig. 4,
Table 1, the aggregate counts, and the Discussion's statement that the solid is "an aggregate of small R and S crystallites,
so a mass from one nucleus is racemic and the run's ee is the vote of about 90 or more crystallites".  I (the writer of this
file) have read that docstring and blind_units.log but NOT the PDF text.  So E2 below is not a blind prediction: its
direction was told to me.  What is new is that each prediction is tied to a named item of the paper that blind_units.py did
not use, with its rule fixed here.  "Used" = Fig. 2, Fig. 4, Table 1, the 24/32 aggregate counts.  Everything else in
asakura_2002.pdf is "unused".  If an item is not in the paper the result is NOT TESTABLE, not a pass.

THE FAILURE.  Used data: at 150 C, 10 runs, n_eff = 272.03, 95% low end 88.33, against 2.4 visible aggregates per run (24 in
10 runs).  A6 needs low end <= 2.4.  Derived from those numbers (script recomputes):
  - n_eff per aggregate at 150 C: 272.0 / 2.4 = 113; low end 88.33 / 2.4 = 36.8.   At 152 C: 8.62 / 3.2 = 2.7.
  - For the low end to reach 2.4, n_eff must be <= 7.39, i.e. rms ee >= 36.8 points; the observed 150 C rms is 6.1.
  - Largest ee^2 among the 150 C runs is (17.2%)^2, so any resample of those 10 runs gives n_eff >= 33.8.
  - To reach n_eff 7.39 with the 7 zero runs, the 7 zeros would have to be +-43.4 points.

CANDIDATE EXPLANATIONS AND PREDICTIONS (each "if the paper gives X").
E1  Aggregates are not units because separately nucleated crystals merge into one visible aggregate (the visible count
    undercounts nuclei).  Prediction P1: the paper describes merging/overlap of separately nucleated masses at 150 C
    (text or caption).  E1 SURVIVES only if it does.  (Used data already cut against it: A5, single-nucleus masses at
    150 C are racemic, ee 0.0 in all 4.  E1 cannot explain a racemic mass grown from ONE nucleus.)
E2  Secondary nucleation inside a mass: a mass from one nucleus is a mosaic of small crystallites of both hands, because
    the melt racemizes and the growth front keeps nucleating either hand.
    P2a: the paper describes the 150 C solid as many small crystallites of both hands within one mass.  HELD if so;
         FAILED if it says the masses are single-handed or single crystals.
    P2b: the paper gives, by an observation independent of the ee spread (micrograph with scale bar, crystallite size,
         or a count), the number of crystallites per run or per aggregate.  HELD if crystallites per aggregate at 150 C
         >= 37 (per run >= 88).  FAILED if below.  If the number is derived from the ee spread, mark CIRCULAR, unscored.
    P2c: crystallites per aggregate (or any speed / fineness measure the paper gives: induction time, growth rate,
         nucleation-site count, crystallite size) is larger/faster/finer at 150 C than at 152 C.  The data need ~113 vs
         ~2.7 effective units per aggregate, a 40-fold change over 2 K.  HELD if the paper's measure goes the same way.
E3  How ee was measured: the 7 zeros are a detection floor, or the ee is from a subsample of the solid.
    P3: the paper states a method and a precision of <= +-5 points on the whole solid.  E3 is REJECTED if so (a floor
        would have to be 43.4 points, and subsampling adds sampling variance to ee^2, which lowers n_eff, the wrong
        direction for A6).  E3 SURVIVES only if the paper's precision is >= 37 points or the solid was heavily
        subsampled in a way that averages hands (a mixed pool of aliquots).
E4  Estimator / heavy tail: n_eff low end from 10 runs of a spiky distribution (7 of 10 at 0) is wrong, so true n_eff
    is small and only a rare big-ee run was missed.
    P4a (derived from used data, no PDF): the minimum n_eff over all resamples of the 10 runs is 33.8, so E4 needs
        runs with |ee| >= 37 points that were not seen.
    P4b: the paper's own fitted zero- or low-stirring distribution (Langevin stationary distribution) has a standard
         deviation <= 10.7 points (n_eff >= 88).  HELD if so.  FAILED if the paper's distribution at 150 C is broader.
