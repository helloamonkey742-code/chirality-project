droplet_sim2.py and exclusion_bak.py predictions, written 2026-10-20 before the first run of either.

Why: droplet_sim.py (run 1) showed the prereg's h <= 3 cut is unfair to drying drops (dry bulk counting, nuclei ~ V^(5/3),
called FAILED in 83% of runs).  A three-agent debate (physicist, statistician, skeptic; two rounds) agreed on the rule
below.  This is a model study of a decision rule, not a test of nature.

Protocol, unchanged from journals/droplet_prereg.md Sections 5-6: pilot 7 x 20 drops at 0.25-16 uL, exp_fit V0, main run
8 x 25 drops at 0.2-1.6 x V0, extension rule (up to two levels), drops with no crystal excluded.

Rule, per run, in this order:
  NS      not scorable (droplet_power.scorable)
  FLAT    the best-fitting curve (COUNT or STEP) changes by less than 0.4 across the levels run
  RISING  Cochran-Armitage trend z > 1.645 (P_single rises with V)
  STEP    L < c*
  SMOOTH  otherwise ("no sharp step")
L = max log-likelihood of COUNT - max log-likelihood of STEP, both with the fixed 3% misread blend.
COUNT: P = F(mu), mu = mu0 (V / V0)^a, a in [0.05, 5/3], F one of
  first  exp(-mu/2)                    (first nucleus fixes the hand, Poisson extras)
  all    2 / (exp(mu/2) + 1)           (Poisson nuclei given at least one)
  dry    2 exp(-mu/2) - exp(-mu)       (no-nucleus drops crystallize once at dryness, single)
STEP: P = Phi(-ln(V / Vc) / s), s in [0.02, 0.6] (s = 0.6 is about the old h = 3 edge; h ~ 1.76 / s).
c* = the lowest 5th percentile of L over every counting cell and condition, 1000 runs per cell on a calibration seed,
then frozen.  Every number below is from a fresh seed, 500 runs per cell.
s* = the largest probit s (of 0.1, 0.15, 0.2, 0.3, 0.45, 0.6) called STEP in >= 95% of runs in every condition.

Counting cells: the three forms at a = 1/3, 2/3, 1, 4/3, 5/3 (P = 1/2 at 3.1 uL; "all" excludes drops with no nucleus,
share exp(-mu)), plus every run-1 model curve (droplet_sim.MODELS, recomputed exactly as run 1) in both its excl and dry
versions.  Step truths: probit s = 0.1, 0.15, 0.2, 0.3, 0.45, 0.6; logistic with h = 1.76 / s for s = 0.15, 0.3;
reported only: a probit s = 0.15 step down to a 0.3 plateau, and a depletion-volume curve (P = 1 below Vd, counting
tail above) with Vd = 0.3 and 0.6 x 3.1 uL.  Conditions: pipette CV 0.05 or 0.15 x misread 0.03 or 0.06 (fit keeps 3%).

droplet_sim2.py predictions
S1  Every counting cell is called STEP in <= 7% of runs in every condition (fresh seed).
S2  Probit steps with s <= 0.15 are called STEP in >= 90% of runs in every condition.
S3  Probit s = 0.6 is called STEP in <= 50% of runs at CV 0.05, misread 0.03.
S4  Logistic s = 0.15 is called STEP in >= 90% of runs at CV 0.05, misread 0.03.
S5  The run-1 curves whose P rises with V (line front w 0.01 B 1, line front w 0.1 B 10, bulk front w 0.01 B 1; excl)
    are called STEP in <= 5% of runs in every condition.
S6  At CV 0.05, misread 0.03 the old h <= 3 rule calls dry a = 5/3 FAILED in more than half of its runs, while the new
    rule calls it STEP in <= 7%.
S7  0.1 <= s* <= 0.2.
S8  -4 <= c* <= -0.5.

exclusion_bak.py: POST HOC.  Bak et al. (2023) data were already read, and the debate agents ran scratch versions of
these fits, so these are expectations, not blind predictions.
Model: each nucleus in a drying drop depletes a zone ell(dt) = sqrt(dt) + nu dt that blocks later nuclei (Johnson-Mehl
thinning), nucleation rate e-folding every t_J; one control number eta = drop radius / zone scale.  Over volume,
eta ~ V^e with e = 1/3 - gamma/2 (3-D drops) or 1/2 - gamma (films), drying time ~ V^gamma, so physical e <= 1/2.
E1  No physical exclusion curve (e <= 1/2; volume, base or ring sites; nu 0, 0.3, 1) has true h > 3 at 0.2-1.6 x V0.
E2  Through the frozen rule (CV 0.05, misread 0.03), every exclusion curve with 0 < e <= 1/2 is called STEP in <= 7%.
E3  Bak Tinc-90 series (11 volumes; n at the P = 1 markers unknown, tried at 3, 6, 10): not STEP for any n, and a step
    with s <= 0.15 is rejected (2 dlogL > 3.84 against the best fit).
E4  The exclusion e-profile on Tinc-90 is best above e = 1/2 and rejects e = 0 by more than 2 log-likelihood units.
